feat: `drainAbortGrace` and `observer` for `createEventBus` (also accepted by `createBroadcastSubscribers`). After `drainTimeout` the bus aborts the handlers still running and, until now, waited for them without limit, so one handler that ignores the abort signal held `stop()` forever. With `drainAbortGrace` (milliseconds, no default; `0` abandons at once) `stop()` gives up on such a handler when the grace ends, and disconnects the adapter without waiting for the adapter to close the subscription of the stuck handler (the Redis, Kafka and NATS adapters only finish closing it when the handler returns; the disconnect ends it). The abandoned handler's later acknowledgement fails; whether its message is delivered again is up to the broker and to whether the handler had already acknowledged it. What was given up on is reported once per `stop()` to `observer` as `{ type: "abandoned", handlers, publishes }` (handlers still running after the grace, publishes still unsettled after `drainPublishTimeout`); an observer that throws or rejects never stops the shutdown. Without the option nothing changes. The total handler budget of the bus is `drainTimeout` + `drainAbortGrace`, added to the transport phase of `shutdown.timeout`; compare it with the kill grace period of your orchestrator. A negative, `NaN` or `Infinity` `drainAbortGrace` throws a `RangeError` from `createEventBus`.
