feat: `drainAbortGrace` and `observer` for `createEventBus` (also accepted by `createBroadcastSubscribers`). After `drainTimeout` the bus aborts the handlers still running and, until now, waited for them without limit, so one handler that ignores the abort signal held `stop()` forever. With `drainAbortGrace` (milliseconds, no default; `0` abandons at once) `stop()` gives up on such a handler when the grace ends, disconnects the adapter, and the handler's later acknowledgement fails so the broker redelivers the message. What was given up on is reported once per `stop()` to `observer` as `{ type: "abandoned", handlers, publishes }` (handlers still running after the grace, publishes still unsettled after `drainPublishTimeout`); an observer that throws or rejects never stops the shutdown. Without the option nothing changes. The total handler budget of the bus is `drainTimeout` + `drainAbortGrace`, added to the transport phase of `shutdown.timeout`; compare it with the kill grace period of your orchestrator. A negative or `NaN` `drainAbortGrace` throws a `RangeError` from `createEventBus`.
