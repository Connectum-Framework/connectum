feat: `ctx.publish` lets a handler that is still running when `EventBus.stop()` begins hand its result on. `stop()` refuses `bus.publish` before it drains the handlers, so the last hop of a relay handler (consume, transform, publish) used to fail during shutdown: on a broker the message was redelivered and, with retry plus a dead-letter queue, could be dead-lettered falsely; with `MemoryAdapter` the next hop was dropped. `ctx.publish(schema, data, options?)` is admitted while the handler of that delivery has not returned and the drain has not been aborted, and `stop()` waits for it (and for the republish of the built-in dead-letter middleware) after the handlers have drained and before the adapter is disconnected, within `drainAbortGrace` when it is set. A call after the handler returned, after the abort, or from an earlier start/stop cycle is rejected. While the bus runs, `ctx.publish` behaves like `bus.publish`. The observer receives `admitted`, `rejected` (`aborted` or `not-in-flight`) and `dlq-republish` while the bus stops. `EventContext` gains a `publish` member, so a hand-written `EventContext` (a test double) must provide it; `createEventContext` accepts it as `publish` in its init and rejects every call when none is given. Handlers that publish through an injected bus keep being refused during `stop()`; rewrite them to use `ctx.publish` to relay during shutdown.
